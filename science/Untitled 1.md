## 1. tRNA Anticodons Pairing with mRNA

During translation, tRNA molecules bring amino acids to the ribosome. Each tRNA has a 3-letter **anticodon** that pairs with a 3-letter **codon** on the mRNA strand.

  

They pair using strict RNA base-pairing rules and align **antiparallel** (opposite directions) to each other:

  

- **Adenine (A)** pairs with **Uracil (U)**
    
      
    
- **Cytosine (C)** pairs with **Guanine (G)**
    
      
    

If an mRNA codon is written 5' → 3', the matching tRNA anticodon reads 3' → 5'.

  

- _Example:_ mRNA codon `5' AUG 3'` pairs with tRNA anticodon `3' UAC 5'`.
    
      
    

## 2. How the DNA Replication Example is Made

Your example:

  

- **Original Strand:** `5' ATG CCG TTA 3'`
    
      
    
- **New Strand:** `3' TAC GGC AAT 5'`
    
      
    

During DNA replication, the enzyme DNA polymerase builds a new strand based on the original template. This happens through two main rules:

  

1. **Complementary Base Pairing:** Adenine (A) always pairs with Thymine (T). Cytosine (C) always pairs with Guanine (G).
    
      
    
2. **Antiparallel Direction:** DNA strands run in opposite directions. The original strand runs 5' to 3', so the new strand must be built 3' to 5'.
    
      
    

Looking at your sequence step-by-step:

  

- `A` pairs with `T`
    
      
    
- `T` pairs with `A`
    
      
    
- `G` pairs with `C`
    
      
    
- `C` pairs with `G`
    
    _(Resulting perfectly in the `TAC GGC AAT` sequence you provided)._
    
      
    

## 3. Decoding Your mRNA Sequence

Your sequence: `5' CAU GCG CAU AUG GCU GUA AG 3'`

  

To find the DNA, codons, and amino acids, we group the mRNA into sets of three letters (codons) starting from the 5' end.

  

There are two types of DNA to be aware of:

  

- **Template Strand (3' → 5'):** The strand of DNA that was actually read to build this mRNA. (A↔T, U↔A, C↔G, G↔C).
    
      
    
- **Coding Strand (5' → 3'):** The opposite DNA strand, which perfectly matches the mRNA (just swapping T for U).
