In molecular biology, **Initiation**, **Elongation**, and **Termination** are the three sequential stages that govern both major steps of gene expression: **Transcription** (copying DNA into RNA) and **Translation** (building a protein from mRNA).

  

## 1. Translation (mRNA $\rightarrow$ Protein)

In translation, the cell decodes the sequence of mRNA codons to assemble a chain of amino acids into a protein.

  

- **Initiation (Assembly):**
    
      
    - The small subunit of the ribosome binds to the mRNA near its $5'$ end and scans until it finds the **start codon (`AUG`)**.
        
          
        
    - An initiator tRNA carrying the amino acid **Methionine** pairs its anticodon (`UAC`) with the start codon.
        
          
        
    - The large ribosomal subunit attaches, placing the initiator tRNA in the **P site** (Peptidyl site) to complete the ribosome setup.
        
          
        
- **Elongation (Chain Building):**
    
      
    - **Codon Recognition:** A new tRNA carrying a specific amino acid enters the empty **A site** (Aminoacyl site) matching the next mRNA codon.
        
          
        
    - **Peptide Bond Formation:** The ribosome catalyzes a peptide bond between the existing amino acid chain in the P site and the new amino acid in the A site.
        
          
        
    - **Translocation:** The ribosome shifts down the mRNA by three bases ($5' \to 3'$). The uncharged tRNA moves to the **E site** (Exit site) and leaves, the growing protein chain shifts to the P site, and the A site is cleared for the next incoming tRNA.
        
          
        
- **Termination (Release):**
    
      
    - Elongation continues until the ribosome reaches a **stop codon** (`UAA`, `UAG`, or `UGA`).
        
          
        
    - Stop codons do not code for an amino acid; instead, a protein called a **release factor** enters the A site.
        
          
        
    - The release factor adds a water molecule to the protein chain, cleaving it from the tRNA. The newly formed polypeptide folds into a protein, and the ribosomal subunits detach.
        
          
        

## 2. Transcription (DNA $\rightarrow$ mRNA)

Before translation can happen, the cell uses these same three stages to copy a gene from DNA into mRNA.

| **Stage**       | **What Happens in Transcription**                                                                                                                         | **Key Molecule**                  |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Initiation**  | **RNA Polymerase** binds to a control region on DNA called a **promoter**. It separates the two DNA strands to expose the template.                       | RNA Polymerase & Promoter         |
| **Elongation**  | RNA Polymerase travels along the DNA template strand ($3' \to 5'$), adding matching RNA nucleotides to build an mRNA strand in the $5' \to 3'$ direction. | Free RNA Nucleotides (A, U, C, G) |
| **Termination** | RNA Polymerase hits a **terminator sequence** on the DNA. The newly built mRNA transcript releases, and RNA Polymerase unbinds from the DNA.              | Terminator sequence               |
